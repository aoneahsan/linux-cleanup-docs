Stale personal files

--stale finds files over about 10 MB in ~/Downloads and configured unprotected roots that have been unused for --days or more, and offers them for deletion one at a time.

linux-cleanup --stale         # or: -p
linux-cleanup --stale -d 180  # only files idle 180+ days
Personal files can be irreplaceable

~/Downloads and your configured unprotected roots hold whatever you put there — including, sometimes, the only copy of something that matters.

The tool cannot tell an installer you will never open again from a document you have not needed yet. It does not try. Read every candidate.

Interactive by policy​

Per-file or per-range confirmation is mandatory. --yes is ignored here and always will be, and this mode is not part of --all-safe. See Guard 3.

The candidate list is printed in full before anything is asked, so running the mode and declining everything is a legitimate way to audit those directories.

What makes a file a candidate​

Three conditions together:

  1. It lives in a configured personal root — by default ~/Downloads; protected roots such as ~/Desktop are ignored
  2. It is larger than roughly 10 MB
  3. Both atime and mtime are older than --days (default 100)

Personal roots are configurable through personal-roots.txt under $XDG_CONFIG_HOME (default ~/.config). See Environment variables.

Why ~/Downloads is not on the protected list

It cannot be — cleaning it is the entire point of this mode. The allowlist protects ~/Documents, ~/Pictures, ~/Music and ~/Videos precisely so that the one directory it does reach is the one you expect.

If you keep things you care about in ~/Downloads, move them somewhere protected — that is the durable fix, and it protects them from more than this tool.

Reading the candidate list​

Each entry shows path, size and idle days. Worth pausing on:

PatternConsider
Installers, ISOs, .deb/.AppImageUsually safe — re-downloadable
Anything you produced rather than downloadedKeep, or move somewhere protected
Client or work deliverablesKeep. Downloads is a bad home for them
Media with no obvious sourceInvestigate before accepting
Archives you never extractedCheck the contents first

A safer sequence​

linux-cleanup --audit            # what is actually large in $HOME
linux-cleanup --stale -d 365     # only genuinely ancient files

Starting at 365 days and working down gives you a short, obvious list first. Once that is clear, lower the threshold.

In the walkthrough​

Step 7 of the guided walkthrough. Skipping it there with s and running it separately later — when you have time to read carefully — is a reasonable habit.
